If you run a startup or an agency and you are ready to **hire an AI automation engineer**, the hardest part is not finding candidates — LinkedIn and Upwork are full of them. The hard part is telling the real engineers apart from the people who took a one-hour Zapier course last week.

I have spent 3+ years building AI automation systems at Techticks for clients across the **USA, UK, Germany, and Australia**. Here is the exact checklist I would give a founder or head of ops before they sign a contract.

## 1. They must have a shipped portfolio you can click

If a candidate cannot send you at least three live client engagements with screenshots, a recorded Loom walkthrough, or a public case study, move on. A serious **AI automation engineer** or **freelance n8n developer** always has shipped work to show. Bonus points if they have a demo workspace you can log into.

## 2. They choose tools based on the problem — not the other way around

A good **workflow automation expert** should be fluent in n8n, Make.com, and Zapier and confidently recommend one over the others based on your use case. Reject anyone who says "n8n for everything" or "just use Zapier" without asking about your operation volume, data sensitivity, and in-house technical capacity.

Quick rule of thumb:

- **Zapier** — fast client delivery, simple linear flows, non-technical ops team, budget for SaaS fees.
- **Make.com** — complex branching, iterators and aggregators, ecommerce and SaaS back-office.
- **n8n (self-hosted)** — high volume, full control, sensitive data, custom JavaScript, long-term cost efficiency.

## 3. They understand webhooks, OAuth, and retries

Serious candidates will talk about webhook signatures, OAuth2 token refresh, idempotency, exponential backoff, and dead-letter queues without flinching. If "retry" means "I will click the rerun button" — that is not a production engineer.

## 4. They have real AI / LLM experience, not just a GPT prompt in a workflow

An **AI agent developer for startups** should be fluent with OpenAI function calling, structured outputs, LangChain or Agno, embeddings and vector stores, and at least one LLM evaluation approach. Ask them to describe a case where a prompt failed in production and what they changed. The answer tells you everything.

## 5. They own the integration stack you actually use

If your stack is HubSpot + Close + Shopify, you want someone who has shipped automations into all three — not just read the docs. Ask for a concrete project on each. CRM automation specialists should know the difference between HubSpot's deals API limits and Close's activity-streams quirks from direct experience.

## 6. They can quote a range without a discovery call

A seasoned freelancer can give you a ballpark in the first message. If they ghost you on budget until a paid call, they are either under-confident or using consulting-theatre tactics. My own standard rates as a freelance n8n developer:

- **Simple automation (2-5 steps, one trigger)**: $150 – $400 fixed, 2-5 days.
- **Medium workflow (CRM ↔ tool sync, LinkedIn outreach)**: $500 – $1500, 1-2 weeks.
- **Complex multi-system AI pipeline**: $1500 – $5000, 2-4 weeks.
- **Retainer for monitoring + new features**: $400 – $1500 / month.

Rates in US / UK / DE / AU markets run 20-40% higher than Upwork average because clients need EU / AEST timezone overlap and SOC-style rigor.

## 7. They document as they ship

Ask: "If you disappeared tomorrow, what would my team get?" The correct answer includes a written runbook, architecture diagram, environment variables list, OAuth app credentials owner, retry policy, and a list of every webhook endpoint.

If the answer is "the workflow itself," walk away. You are buying a liability.

## 8. They know what NOT to automate

A good **business process automation consultant** will push back on at least one of your ideas. If every request gets an immediate "yes, I can automate that" — they are selling you busywork.

Things that are usually a bad ROI to automate: approvals that change weekly, one-off data migrations, anything governed by humans who like to negotiate, and low-frequency tasks below ~5 minutes per week.

## 9. They can migrate you off vendor lock-in

Any serious AI automation engineer should be able to migrate your Zapier or Make.com stack to n8n when the SaaS bill gets out of hand. Ask them how they approach **Zapier-to-n8n migration** — the answer should include an inventory step, a risk-ranked cutover plan, a parallel-run period, and rollback.

## 10. They respect your timezone

For USA / UK / DE / AU clients: if you need live troubleshooting during your working hours, confirm the overlap. I am based in Pakistan (GMT+5) and regularly take calls in US-East evening, UK/DE mornings, and AEST mornings — but always confirm.

## Red flags

- Candidate cannot show a self-hosted n8n instance or Make.com scenario in a real workspace
- "I only use Zapier because it is easiest" (for a production ops engagement)
- No version control, no environment-variable hygiene, no staging environment
- Promises 100% accuracy on LLM outputs without mentioning evals or human-in-the-loop
- Refuses to sign an NDA or talk about data handling
- Wants full payment upfront with no milestones
